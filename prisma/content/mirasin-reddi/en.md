An inheritance can consist of debts as well as assets. An heir who does not want to be personally liable, with their own assets, for the deceased's debts may disclaim the inheritance within the period set by law. This article summarises, for general information, the disclaimer of inheritance and the three-month deadline.

## What disclaiming an inheritance means

Disclaiming an inheritance is the act by which a legal or appointed heir declares to the court that they do not wish to accept the inheritance, thereby losing heir status. The disclaimed share passes to the other heirs, or to the next heirs in the statutory order, as if the disclaiming heir had died before the deceased.

## The three-month disclaimer period

Under article 606 of Turkish Civil Code No. 4721, an heir may disclaim the inheritance **within three months** of learning of the death of the person they inherit from. This period is a strict, non-extendable deadline; once it has passed, the right to disclaim can no longer, as a rule, be exercised, and the inheritance is deemed accepted. The period starts not from the date of death itself, but from the date the heir **learns** of the death - so different heirs of the same deceased may have periods starting on different dates.

## How to disclaim

The inheritance is disclaimed by an oral or written, unconditional declaration made to the civil court of peace at the deceased's last place of residence. The court records the declaration in minutes and registers the disclaimer.

## Who may disclaim

Both statutory heirs (descendants, spouse, parents, etc.) and heirs appointed by the deceased's will may disclaim the inheritance. For heirs lacking capacity to act, or under guardianship, disclaimer is made by their legal representative and, where required, with the permission of the civil court of peace or the court of first instance.

## Deemed disclaimer by operation of law

If it is obviously evident or officially established that the deceased was insolvent at the date of death, the inheritance is deemed disclaimed by operation of law, without any separate declaration - this is known as "deemed disclaimer." In practice, however, whether insolvency is "obviously evident" is often disputed, so prudent heirs generally still prefer to make an explicit disclaimer declaration within the period.

## Common mistakes

- Miscalculating the date the death was learned of, or missing the three-month deadline.
- Engaging in conduct that implies acceptance of the inheritance (such as selling or using an asset from the estate) before investigating the deceased's debt situation, since such conduct can forfeit the right to disclaim.
- Assuming deemed disclaimer applies automatically to every insolvent estate and therefore failing to file an explicit disclaimer in time.
- Attempting to disclaim on behalf of a minor or an heir under guardianship without obtaining the required court permission.

## Competent court

A disclaimer declaration is made to the civil court of peace at the deceased's last place of residence. Because the start of the disclaimer period and the date an heir is deemed to have learned of the death depend on the specific facts, this article guarantees no particular outcome; if there is any doubt about the deadline calculation, prompt legal advice is recommended.
