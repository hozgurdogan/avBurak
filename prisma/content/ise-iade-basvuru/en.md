For an employee whose employment contract has been terminated and who believes that termination was invalid, the first question is usually: how much time do I have, and what is the first step? This article summarises, for general information, the time limit for a reinstatement claim, the mandatory mediation stage, and the procedural mistakes seen most often.

## Who can claim reinstatement

To benefit from the job-security provisions of Labour Law No. 4857, the workplace must employ thirty or more employees and the employee must have at least six months' seniority; the employee must also be working under an indefinite-term contract and must not hold a position as the employer's representative. Where these conditions are met and the employment contract was terminated without a valid reason, or the reason given does not reflect the truth, the employee has the right to bring a reinstatement claim.

## The one-month deadline to apply for mediation

Under article 20 of Law No. 4857, an employee whose contract has been terminated must apply to a mediator **within one month** of the date the termination notice was served. This period is a strict, non-extendable deadline; failing to apply within it generally forfeits the right to claim reinstatement. Mediation is a mandatory precondition set by law before a reinstatement lawsuit can be filed - applying directly to the court is not possible.

## How the mediation process works

The mediation application is made through the Ministry of Justice's mediation system, and an impartial mediator is assigned. The parties meet before the mediator to seek agreement; if they settle, matters such as reinstatement, compensation for non-reinstatement, and pay for the period out of work are recorded in the settlement minutes. If no agreement is reached, a lawsuit may be filed before the labour court **within two weeks** of the date the final minutes are drawn up.

## Common mistakes

- Miscalculating the date the termination notice was served, or missing the one-month deadline.
- Overlooking the two-week deadline to file suit after the final mediation minutes are drawn up.
- Confusing a reinstatement claim with severance/notice pay claims - these rest on different legal grounds and are generally subject to different limitation periods.
- Failing to keep the documents showing whether the termination notice was in writing, with reasons given, and how it was served (delivery receipt, e-mail, minutes).

## Competent court and possible outcomes

If no settlement is reached in mediation, the resulting lawsuit is heard before the labour court. If the court finds the termination invalid, the employer must reinstate the employee within one month of the judgment becoming final, or pay compensation for non-reinstatement; the employee is also paid wages and other entitlements for the period out of work, up to a maximum of four months. The outcome of any case depends on its own concrete evidence; this article guarantees no particular result.
