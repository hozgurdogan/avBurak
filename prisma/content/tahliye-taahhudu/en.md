A vacation commitment, frequently relied on by landlords, is a document that can be held invalid if it is not obtained at the right time and in the right way. This article summarises, for general information, the conditions for a valid vacation commitment and the eviction routes based on it.

## What a vacation commitment is

A vacation commitment is a document in which the tenant undertakes in writing to vacate the leased property by a specific date. It is regulated in article 352(1) of Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098 and gives the landlord a quick eviction route if the property is not vacated by the committed date.

## The decisive condition: when the commitment was signed

For a vacation commitment to be valid, it must be signed of the tenant's own free will **after the lease has already been formed**. A vacation commitment taken together with the lease agreement, or before it, is consistently held invalid in the case law of the Court of Cassation, on the reasoning that a tenant who has not yet signed the lease is not in a position to refuse the landlord's request. It is therefore important not only what date the commitment bears, but that the date it was actually signed can be proven.

## The deadline to apply for eviction

If the tenant does not vacate the property by the committed date, the landlord may seek eviction through enforcement or through litigation **within one month** of that date. This one-month period is also a strict, non-extendable deadline; once it has passed, the vacation commitment can no longer be relied on.

## Two eviction routes

- **Eviction through enforcement:** the landlord applies to the enforcement office to start an enforcement proceeding based on the vacation commitment; if the tenant does not object within seven days, eviction is carried out by the enforcement officer.
- **Eviction through litigation:** the landlord can reach the same result by filing an eviction action before the civil court of peace. If the tenant objects, eviction through enforcement can also turn into litigation.

## Common mistakes

- Having the tenant sign the vacation commitment on the same date as the lease, or before it.
- Missing the one-month deadline to apply, counted from the date stated in the commitment.
- Failing to prepare evidence of whether the commitment was handwritten or a pre-printed form, and that the signature belongs to the tenant.
- Where there is more than one tenant, not checking whether the commitment was signed by all of them.

## Competent authority

An enforcement proceeding based on a vacation commitment is started at the enforcement office where the leased property is located; an eviction action is filed before the civil court of peace. The validity of the commitment and the outcome of the process depend on concrete elements such as the date and content of the document; this article guarantees no particular outcome.
