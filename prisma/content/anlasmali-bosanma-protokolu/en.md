An uncontested divorce is a relatively fast route in which the couple agree on the consequences of the divorce; but how quickly it concludes depends on the divorce protocol being complete and legally sound. This article summarises, for general information, the elements a divorce protocol should contain and how the process works.

## Conditions for an uncontested divorce

Under article 166(3) of Turkish Civil Code No. 4721, an uncontested divorce requires that the marriage has lasted **at least one year**, and that the spouses apply jointly, or that one spouse accepts the action filed by the other. The judge must hear the parties in person and be satisfied that their intentions are freely expressed, and must also approve the protocol that has been prepared. Where the judge considers it necessary, changes may be made to the protocol.

## Elements a protocol should contain

A complete uncontested-divorce protocol should contain clear, workable provisions between the parties on the following:

1. **Intent to divorce** — an explicit statement that the parties agree to divorce by mutual consent.
2. **Custody and contact** — where there is a joint child, who is awarded custody and how contact with the other parent (visiting days, holiday periods) will be arranged.
3. **Child support** — the amount the non-custodial parent will contribute toward the child's expenses and how it will be paid.
4. **Post-divorce alimony** — where one spouse requests it and the other accepts, the amount, duration and method of payment (or an explicit statement that no such alimony is claimed).
5. **Material and moral compensation** — any amount one spouse claims from the other and its acceptance.
6. **Division of matrimonial property** — the parties' agreement regarding joint assets, household goods and any real property; or, if this is not addressed, a statement that division of the property regime is left outside the protocol.
7. **Signatures and date** — the protocol signed before the judge at the hearing, or a previously signed protocol confirmed at the hearing.

## How the process works

The parties apply to the family court together with the protocol they have prepared. At the hearing, the judge hears the parties separately or together to be satisfied their intentions are sound and free; the judge reviews the protocol and may request changes to provisions found contrary to the child's interests (particularly on custody and support). If the parties accept the change, the divorce is granted; if not, the case may turn into a contested divorce.

## Common mistakes

- Stating alimony and compensation amounts vaguely or inconsistently.
- Drafting custody and contact arrangements that are not workable or not suited to the child's age and needs.
- Leaving division of the matrimonial property out of the protocol entirely without anticipating that a separate action could follow later.
- Having one party draft the protocol alone before the hearing and sign it without the other's genuine consent.

## Competent court

An uncontested divorce action is filed in the family court of either spouse's place of residence; where there is no family court, jurisdiction belongs to the civil court of first instance. The process can vary with how complete the preparation is and what the parties state at the hearing; this article guarantees no particular outcome.
