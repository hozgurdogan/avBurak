The acquisition of immovable property in Türkiye by foreign nationals is governed by article 35 of Land Registry Law no. 2644 and the secondary legislation issued under it. The process is not simply a matter of paying a purchase price: a chain of administrative checks, a mandatory valuation and a set of currency formalities must be completed before registration in the land registry. This article sets out that chain in sequence, for general information.

## Who may acquire

The condition of reciprocity was removed from article 35 by an amendment in 2012, and acquisition is open to nationals of the countries determined by Presidential decision. The statutory limits nevertheless remain:

- A foreign natural person may acquire at most thirty hectares of immovable property in the country as a whole.
- Acquisitions by foreign natural persons may not exceed ten per cent of the surface area of any given district.
- For property falling within military prohibited zones, military security zones or special security zones, a letter of suitability must be obtained from the relevant command.

The regime for foreign companies is different. Legal persons of foreign nationality may acquire immovable property only within the framework of special legislation. By contrast, a company incorporated in Türkiye under the Turkish Commercial Code is treated as a Turkish company even if its capital is foreign, and its acquisitions are assessed under the provisions introduced by Law no. 6491. Structuring an investment through a Turkish company therefore has consequences not only for tax but for the capacity to acquire in the first place.

## Type of title and the status of the building

The first document a purchaser should examine is the title deed itself. In a project built on a land share, title may be registered either as **construction servitude** (kat irtifakı) or as **condominium ownership** (kat mülkiyeti). A construction servitude is a right established over a building that is not yet complete; condominium ownership is full ownership, established once the occupancy permit has been issued. When acquiring property held under a construction servitude, the permit status of the project, the completion timetable and the allocation of responsibility for obtaining the occupancy permit should all be reflected in the contract.

Annotations, declarations and encumbrances on the register must also be examined. A mortgage, an attachment, an annotation of a promise to sell, a family residence annotation or an expropriation declaration each affects the purchaser's position directly. Zoning status and the building permit should be confirmed with the municipality at the same stage.

## The mandatory valuation report

Since 2019, transfers of immovable property to which a foreign national is a party require a **valuation report** prepared by an institution authorised by the Capital Markets Board or the General Directorate of Land Registry and Cadastre. The report is submitted to the land registry directorate and has a limited period of validity. Because a discrepancy between the declared sale price and the value stated in the report has consequences both for the transfer tax and for any subsequent tax review, the price recorded should reflect the genuine value.

## Payment, currency rules and charges

Where the purchase price is transferred from abroad, it is important in practice to have the bank issue a **Foreign Currency Purchase Document** (Döviz Alım Belgesi). It serves both to evidence the source of the funds and, where an application for citizenship is contemplated, to prove that the minimum investment threshold has been met.

Transfer tax is payable at the statutory rate by the purchaser and the seller separately; an agreement between them to the contrary does not affect their liability towards the administration. A revolving fund service charge is also collected. For dwellings and workplaces, a **compulsory earthquake insurance** (DASK) policy is among the documents required for registration.

## The transaction at the land registry

A transfer becomes valid only before the land registry directorate, upon execution of the official deed. A contract concluded between the parties before a notary does not transfer ownership; what may be done before a notary is a **promise-to-sell agreement**, which, once annotated on the register, allows the purchaser to assert a personal right against third parties. Where a dwelling is sold off-plan, the formal requirements of consumer legislation apply in addition.

A sworn interpreter must be present for any party who does not speak Turkish. A purchaser who cannot attend in person may be represented under a power of attorney executed abroad and apostilled; the property and the authority granted must be identified expressly in the instrument.

## Pre-transfer review

A pre-transfer review is effective when it is completed before contract negotiations rather than after. In practice it covers at least the following: a full and current extract of the register together with every annotation, declaration and encumbrance on it; the zoning status and building permit; whether the occupancy permit has been obtained; the management plan and any outstanding service charges; obligations arising from resolutions of the condominium owners' assembly; whether property tax is in arrears; where the property is occupied by a tenant, the term of the lease and the conditions for vacating; and whether anything restricts the seller's power of disposal. The results of that review perform a protective function once they are reflected in the payment schedule and in the representations and warranties given in the contract.

## The law applicable to succession

Under the Act on Private International and Procedural Law no. 5718, succession to immovable property located in Türkiye is governed by the law of the place where the property is situated — that is, by Turkish law. The rule applies irrespective of the nationality or last domicile of the deceased. Reserved-portion rules, the certificate of inheritance and the transfer procedure therefore follow Turkish law. Whether a will executed abroad can take effect in respect of Turkish immovable property requires a separate examination as to form and content. The decision whether to acquire in the name of an individual or through a company therefore has consequences for succession as well as for tax.

## Obligations after acquisition

After registration, the property tax liability must be notified to the relevant municipality, utility subscriptions transferred, and income tax obligations monitored where rental income is generated. For persons not resident in Türkiye, the declaration of rental income and the application of double taxation treaties should be assessed separately. On a subsequent disposal, the time elapsed since acquisition determines how the capital gain is treated.

## Assessment

The points at which these transactions stall are rarely to do with payment. They cluster instead around a missing occupancy permit, unresolved zoning issues, annotations on the register, or delay in the military zone suitability letter. For that reason the payment schedule and the administrative timetable should be tied together in the contract, with express provision for the stage at which each instalment falls due and the documents that must be produced against it.
