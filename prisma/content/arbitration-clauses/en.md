In a cross-border contract, the dispute resolution clause is usually reached in the last half hour of the negotiation. Yet it is the clause that determines how the rest of the contract will be enforced once a dispute arises. What follows sets out, for general information, the matters that arise when an arbitration clause is drafted in a contract connected with Turkish law.

## The applicable framework

Arbitration in Türkiye is governed by International Arbitration Law no. 4686 where the dispute contains a foreign element, and by the arbitration provisions of Civil Procedure Law no. 6100 where it does not. The International Arbitration Law was drafted on the basis of the UNCITRAL Model Law, so its structure is familiar to parties accustomed to international practice.

For the enforcement of foreign arbitral awards in Türkiye, the governing instrument is the 1958 New York Convention. Türkiye is a party to it and has entered two reservations: the Convention applies only to awards made in contracting states, and only to disputes arising out of relationships considered commercial under Turkish law. Where the Convention does not apply, enforcement proceeds under the Act on Private International and Procedural Law no. 5718.

## Arbitrability

Not every dispute may be referred to arbitration. The Civil Procedure Law provides that matters over which the parties cannot freely dispose, and disputes concerning rights in rem over immovable property located in Türkiye, are not arbitrable. Limitations also apply in employment, family law, insolvency and certain matters falling within administrative jurisdiction. When an arbitration clause is included in a contract concerning a real estate investment, it is therefore important to separate proprietary claims relating to the transfer of ownership from contractual claims between the parties.

## The minimum content of the clause

A workable arbitration clause settles the following, beyond argument:

- **Scope.** A wide formulation covering all disputes "arising out of or in connection with" the contract is preferable. Narrow formulations invite argument that claims founded in tort or unjust enrichment fall outside the clause.
- **Institutional or ad hoc.** Where institutional arbitration is chosen, the institution must be named in full and without ambiguity. The Istanbul Arbitration Centre (ISTAC), the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC) and comparable institutions publish their own model clauses, and departing from them is rarely advisable.
- **The seat.** The seat is a legal concept, distinct from the place where hearings are physically held. The choice of seat determines the court competent to hear an application to set aside and the procedural law that governs the arbitration. If Istanbul is chosen as the seat, setting-aside proceedings are heard by the Turkish courts.
- **Number of arbitrators and the method of appointment.** A sole arbitrator makes a material difference to time and cost in small and mid-sized disputes. A three-member tribunal rests on each party appointing one arbitrator.
- **Language.** Absent agreement, the language is determined by the tribunal. A divergence between the language of the contract and the language of the arbitration generates translation cost and difficulty in the presentation of evidence.
- **The law governing the substance.** The law governing the arbitration agreement and the law governing the substance of the contract may in theory differ; stating both expressly forecloses the argument.

## Separability and competence-competence

An arbitration clause is independent of the contract in which it is contained. The invalidity of the main contract does not, as a rule, affect the clause. Correspondingly, the tribunal has the power to rule on its own jurisdiction. Together these two principles prevent a party from escaping arbitration by asserting that the contract is void.

## Multi-tier clauses and mandatory mediation

Multi-tier clauses making negotiation or mediation a condition precedent to arbitration are common in practice. Such clauses need to be definite as to period and procedure; a provision that the parties "shall discuss in good faith" invites a dispute about whether the condition has been satisfied.

Under Turkish law, mediation is a procedural precondition for a significant proportion of commercial claims. That rule concerns the state courts. Where a structure makes it possible for part of a dispute to go to arbitration and part to the courts, the way the mediation stage will operate should be settled at the outset.

## Interim relief

The International Arbitration Law provides that a tribunal may grant interim injunctions and interim attachment, but that jurisdiction lies with the courts in respect of measures requiring enforcement by an execution office or another public authority. It is therefore sensible to include a sentence confirming that an application to a court for interim relief is not to be treated as a waiver of the agreement to arbitrate.

## Setting aside and enforcement

An action to set aside an international arbitral award rendered in Türkiye must be brought within thirty days of notification of the award. The grounds are exhaustively listed, and no review on the merits is available. On enforcement of a foreign award, the court does not re-examine the merits either; its review is confined to matters such as the validity of the arbitration agreement, whether the party was given an opportunity to present its case, the scope of the award and conformity with public policy.

## Time, cost and confidentiality

The assumption that arbitration is faster than litigation depends on the size of the dispute and the structure chosen. The International Arbitration Law requires the tribunal to render its award within one year unless the parties agree otherwise, and institutional rules generally impose time limits of their own. Against that, in a three-member tribunal the arbitrators' fees, institutional charges and expert costs can make the total exceed the cost of litigation in low-value disputes. Some contracts therefore adopt a threshold providing for a sole arbitrator below a stated amount.

Confidentiality is not an automatic consequence of arbitration. While hearings are not public, the confidentiality of the proceedings and of the award is secured only where the contract or the chosen institutional rules provide for it expressly. It should also be borne in mind that confidentiality may be curtailed if the file reaches the state courts at the setting-aside or enforcement stage.

## Defective clauses seen in practice

The clauses that generate the most argument are these: clauses referring to an arbitral institution that does not exist or is misnamed; contradictory clauses providing simultaneously for arbitration and for the exclusive jurisdiction of a state court; asymmetric clauses giving only one party the option between arbitration and litigation; and clauses that fix neither the seat nor the governing law. These defects usually cannot be cured once a dispute has arisen, because the other party no longer has any reason to consent to the correction.

## Assessment

An arbitration clause is not insurance for the contract; it is the procedural rule that takes effect if a dispute occurs. Where the institution, seat, language, number of arbitrators and governing law are each decided deliberately, the first stage of a dispute begins with the argument on the merits. Where they are left open, the first stage is spent arguing about the arbitration clause itself.
